# 1 Introduction

Most LES practitioners close the subgrid stress with an eddy viscosity, and in doing so they assume that energy flows only from the resolved scales to the subgrid scales. A priori tests on filtered DNS data have long shown that this assumption is incomplete: locally, energy also flows back to the resolved scales. Because a positive eddy viscosity is purely dissipative, the model cannot represent this backscatter, and where backscatter is strong the closure drains energy from scales that are in fact gaining it. Figure 1 makes the mismatch visible: the modeled flux is forward everywhere, while the filtered-DNS flux changes sign across the domain.

Two lines of work have addressed backscatter, and each leaves the same question open. Stochastic models add a random forcing whose level is set to match an average backscatter rate; matching an average, however, says little about where reverse transfer occurs. Dynamic procedures can return a negative eddy viscosity, but the resulting instability is commonly removed by clipping or averaging, which discards the very events at issue. Neither approach tells a modeler where their closure is wrong.

This paper gives modelers a tool for answering that question: a local diagnostic that measures the sign and size of interscale energy transfer at each point of a filtered field. Applied to filtered DNS of decaying isotropic turbulence, it shows where an eddy-viscosity closure removes energy that the flow is gaining, so modelers can test their closure where it is most likely to fail. Section 2 defines the diagnostic; Section 3 applies it.
